Featured project

Self-Balancing Robot

A two-wheeled robot that stays upright on its own by constantly driving its wheels under its centre of mass, the same idea behind a Segway. I designed and 3D-printed the chassis, wired the electronics and wrote the control software from scratch.

The challenge

My robot is tall, so its centre of mass sits high. That makes it slower to fall, but it also means small sensor errors and motor differences quickly turn into drifting or tipping over.

How I solved it

  • Sensor fusion: a complementary filter combines the gyroscope and accelerometer of the MPU-6050 to get a clean tilt angle, updated 200 times per second.
  • PID control: converts tilt error into motor power, with integral anti-windup and a fall cutoff at 35°.
  • Drift correction: an outer speed loop and an auto-trim slowly shift the balance point so the robot stops creeping across the floor.
  • Live tuning tool: I built a serial command interface to change PID gains, motor trims and the balance point while the robot runs, then save them to EEPROM.
  • Motor deadband compensation so the cheap DC motors respond to small corrections.
